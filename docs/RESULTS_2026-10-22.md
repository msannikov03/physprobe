# Do frozen video encoders and a generative video tokenizer see hidden object physics? Results (22 Oct 2026)

Mikhail Sannikov, Ilya Mikhalchuk. Course project, Modern Methods and Algorithms of Generative AI (Skoltech, 2026).

**Question.** A visually identical cube is pushed with a fixed impulse; its mass m and friction coefficient μ change only how it moves. Do frozen video representations encode m and μ linearly, and does a touch signal add to them? **Answer.** Yes for vision: the initial speed (hence mass, because the impulse is fixed) and the deceleration (hence friction) are linearly readable from every clip-level input that keeps the cube's trajectory, with V-JEPA 2 at test R² 0.997 for ln m and 0.977 for μ, and controls showing that the probe reads kinematics, not artefacts. But raw pixels do as well (0.999 / 0.982), so this clean scene does not rank encoders. The physics in the latent of a generative video tokenizer (Cosmos) is less linearly decodable at the poolings we tried (best 0.943 / 0.682; 0.748 for μ in the second implementation). A realistic touch signal without absolute force scale reaches only 0.521 / 0.452 on its own; video adds a large margin over it, and touch adds little on top of V-JEPA 2 (+0.005 for μ) and more on top of the weaker DINOv2 sequence (+0.11; +0.08 in the second implementation).

Canonical numbers: `results/{push,slide,poscheck}/` (`table.md`, `gates.md`, `probes.csv`, `figs/`), produced by `scripts/reproduce.sh`. A second, independent implementation of the probes (`scripts/quicklook_probes.py`, `results/quicklook/`) is run as a cross-check; see "Protocol sensitivity". This version replaces an internal draft of the same day; the changes are listed at the end. Hashes inside results/, the feature info and the notebook refer to the private development history that was squashed into the public v1.0 commit d41bcfa; its physprobe/ and tests/ trees are identical. The data were generated by the simulator code first committed as a124216 (physics identical to the release).

## Setup

**Scene.** MuJoCo 3.16, a 10 cm red cube on a grey plane, fixed top-down camera (1.5 m field of view, cube starts 12 % from the left edge), 64 frames at 64 fps, 256×256 RGB. Hidden parameters per clip: mass m ~ log-uniform [0.5, 2] kg, friction μ ~ uniform [0.2, 0.8]. The cube looks identical in every clip; only its motion depends on (m, μ). Friction is Coulomb dry friction μ·m·g (MuJoCo `dof_frictionloss`; native contact friction made the fast cube hop), so the deceleration is exactly μg while sliding and the stopping distance is v₀²/(2μg) up to the integrator's offset v₀·dt/2 (≤ 0.8 mm).

**Scenarios.** `push`: a fixed impulse J = 1 N·s gives v₀ = J/m, so both m and μ are identifiable (n = 2000). `slide`: fixed v₀ = 1.2 m/s, so only μ is identifiable and any recovered mass is a leak (n = 1000, negative control). `poscheck`: static cube at a random position (n = 300), to check that pooled features keep position. Frame 0 is rendered at rest before the impulse and is pixel-identical across all push and slide clips, so a feature of frame 0 cannot carry (m, μ). Every clip also has the floor contact signal [normal force, tangential force, slip speed] at 64 Hz and the true centre-of-mass state.

**Representations (frozen, no fine-tuning).** V-JEPA 2 ViT-L (`facebook/vjepa2-vitl-fpc64-256`, a predictive, non-generative self-supervised video model; native 64×256×256 input, no crop; last layer and blocks 8 and 16). DINOv2-base per frame (256→224, [CLS ‖ mean patch]). Random-init copies of both as architecture controls. The latent of a generative video tokenizer, NVIDIA Cosmos-Tokenize1 CV8×8×8 (continuous, 16 channels, 8× temporal and 8×8 spatial compression: the 64 frames plus the last one repeated give a (16, 9, 32, 32) latent; TorchScript encoder in bf16, raw latents). This tokenizer is the only component of a generative model in the study. Pretrained encoders run in bf16; the random-init controls run in fp32 with TF32 off, because bf16 rounding noise is as large as a random network's between-clip variance. Pooling modes: `static` (frame 0), `last` (frame 63, DINOv2), `clip_mean` (mean over all tokens), `clip_seq` (V-JEPA 2: spatial mean in each of 32 temporal slots; DINOv2: 16 frames; Cosmos: 9 slots), and for Cosmos `clip_grid4` (4×4 spatial grid per slot).

**Baselines from the same clips.** Grey 32×32 pixels (8 temporal bins; frame 0; last frame). A colour tracker (cube centroid per frame). The true x(t), unquantised: the linear x(t) read-out reference, a ceiling for μ but not for ln m, which is nonlinear in x(t). The same x(t) quantised to 1 px (informational). [ln v₀, a] fitted to the unquantised x(t). Raw contact, an oracle by construction (N = mg, Fₜ/N = μ). "Realistic" contact: forces below 10⁻³ of the clip maximum set to zero, divided by that maximum, plus additive N(0, 0.05) noise, and a binary slip flag, so no absolute force scale survives. A noise curve on the realistic block: additive N(0, p) on all three channels, p = 0, 0.1, 0.3, 1.0.

**Probes (canonical protocol, CONTRACT v1.3).** Ridge regression on z-scored features, PCA to 256 components when the input has more than 2048 dims, α ∈ 10⁻³…10⁶ chosen by 5-fold CV on train only; targets ln m and μ; canonical split 70/15/15 by seed plus two reshuffles; test R² mean ± std over the three splits; mode and layer per representation chosen on val. Combined inputs (video + realistic contact) z-score each block and scale it by 1/√D. Controls: mean predictor, label shuffle, and a 100-permutation null (p95 ≤ max(0.10, 3.3/√n_train)).

## What it says

1. **Physics is linearly readable from frozen video features.** V-JEPA 2 gives R² 0.997 for ln m and 0.977 for μ: the probe reads the initial speed v₀ (mass) and the deceleration μg (friction) from the trajectory. The controls show it is kinematics, not artefacts: nothing is recoverable from the identical first frame, no mass is recovered when v₀ is fixed, and V-JEPA 2's pooled features still encode position. "Mass" here means v₀ = J/m and is identifiable only because the impulse J is fixed and known; with an unknown push, mass could not be recovered from video alone.
2. **Raw pixels do just as well in this clean scene** (0.982 vs 0.977 for μ, 0.999 vs 0.997 for ln m), replicating CALIPER's observation (see "Ours vs CALIPER"). A fixed camera and a single object make the task a trajectory read-out, so this scene does not rank representations: differences of 0.005 are smaller than what the probe protocol alone changes. CALIPER measured that representations separate only under visual nuisances (lighting, texture, camera jitter, distractors); we did not test nuisances.
3. **A single frame, or a frame average, carries about one number: where the cube stopped.** Every cube is at rest by frame 63, so the last frame shows only the stopping distance d = v₀²/(2μg) = J²/(2μgm²), that is ln d = const − 2 ln m − ln μ. Under our priors Var(2 ln m) = 0.64 is four times Var(ln μ) = 0.15, so d mostly reflects mass. A Monte Carlo over the priors gives R² 0.83 (ln m) and 0.24 (μ) as the best any function of d alone can reach, and pixels' last frame sits at that bound (0.819 / 0.205). DINOv2's last frame (0.815 / 0.191) sits at the same bound, and its average over frames, `clip_mean` (0.838 / 0.176), at the matching bound for the mean position over the clip (0.84 / 0.22 by the same Monte Carlo): averaging per-frame features over time keeps about one scalar, the final or mean position. Separating m from μ requires the time profile, the initial speed from the first frames and the deceleration from when the cube stops. Inputs that keep it reach the top of the table: pixels in 8 time bins (0.999 / 0.982), DINOv2 as a 16-frame sequence (0.983 / 0.842), and V-JEPA 2, whose spatio-temporal tokens keep 0.970 for μ even after global mean pooling. Mid-network V-JEPA 2 features (block 16: 0.977) are marginally better than the last layer (0.970), a gap smaller than the protocol sensitivity.
4. **Pretraining helps, and how much depends on the pooling.** Gains in R²(μ) on push at the same pooling and PCA width, pretrained minus the same architecture with random weights:

   | encoder, pooling | dims (PCA) | pretrained | random init | gain |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | V-JEPA 2, clip_mean | 1024 (none) | 0.970 | 0.850 | +0.12 |
   | V-JEPA 2, clip_seq | 32768 (256) | 0.967 | 0.883 | +0.08 |
   | DINOv2, last frame | 1536 (none) | 0.191 | 0.180 | +0.01 |
   | DINOv2, clip_mean | 1536 (none) | 0.176 | 0.169 | +0.01 |
   | DINOv2, clip_seq | 24576 (256) | 0.842 | 0.684 | +0.16 |

   With 512 PCA components (quick-look implementation) the V-JEPA 2 `clip_seq` gain is +0.04, so the size of the gain depends on the probe protocol, while its sign does not. DINOv2's last-frame and frame-mean rows sit at the single-scalar bound of point 3 whether pretrained or not, so they say little about pretraining. Random spatio-temporal features keep much of the pixel information (random V-JEPA 2 `clip_seq`: 0.997 for ln m), which is why the random controls are far from zero.
5. **Touch: exact contact is an oracle, realistic contact is weak, and video adds a large margin over it.** Exact floor contact gives R² 1.000 for both targets by construction (N = mg, Fₜ/N = μ), and 0.971 for ln m even on slide, where vision correctly finds nothing. The realistic signal removes the absolute scale. On its own it reaches 0.521 for ln m and 0.452 for μ: what survives normalisation is essentially the stop time t_stop = J/(mμg), one number that mixes m and μ. Under our priors any function of t_stop alone reaches at most 0.535 / 0.471, and the noise-free block (0.527 / 0.457) sits on that bound, which is also the best evidence that no leak remains. Video beats it by a wide margin (V-JEPA 2 block 16: 0.997 / 0.977). Adding touch to video helps in proportion to what the video input lacks: V-JEPA 2 block 16 0.977 → 0.982 and V-JEPA 2 `clip_seq` 0.967 → 0.975 (little), DINOv2 `clip_seq` 0.842 → 0.948 (+0.11, because the stop time that touch supplies is what the per-frame sequence reads poorly; +0.08 in the quick-look implementation, 0.891 → 0.972). Noise curve on the realistic block (additive N(0, p) on every channel, no other noise):

   | p | 0 | 0.1 | 0.3 | 1.0 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | R²(ln m) | 0.527 | 0.519 | 0.499 | 0.378 |
   | R²(μ) | 0.457 | 0.450 | 0.434 | 0.336 |

   **Correction.** An internal draft of this document reported realistic contact alone at 0.95 (ln m) and 0.86 (μ), nearly as good as vision. That was a leak. After the cube stops, MuJoCo leaves a friction residual of about 10⁻⁸ N, proportional to m; divided by the per-clip maximum μmg and z-scored on train, it became an exact 1/μ code. The block now sets every force below 10⁻³ of the clip maximum to zero before normalising, and the conclusion reverses: in this scene vision carries the physics, and touch without absolute scale is weak on its own. That touch adds little on top of the best video input still holds. Its value where vision is degraded (visual nuisances, short clips) is untested. The floor is now guarded by `tests/test_pipeline.py` (`test_realistic_contact_floor`, with a no-floor negative control that must show the leak).
6. **The generative tokenizer's latent carries the physics, but it is less linearly decodable at the poolings we tried.** Cosmos latents pooled to a 4×4 grid per time slot give R² 0.943 for ln m and 0.682 for μ, against 0.999 / 0.982 for pixels and 0.997 / 0.977 for V-JEPA 2. The pooled modes keep little position (poscheck x₀: 0.158 for the 16-dim global mean, 0.166 for the per-slot spatial mean) yet most of the mass signal (ln m 0.908 from the spatial means, 0.680 from the global mean), so the latent channels encode motion directly, not only through where the cube is. The 4×4 layout adds mainly friction (μ 0.413 → 0.682) and position (0.166 → 0.986). Nothing leaks on slide (ln m −0.024 to −0.025 in all three modes). The comparison is not at matched spatial resolution: pixels keep 32×32 per time bin, while Cosmos is pooled from its native 32×32 latent to 1×1 or 4×4 cells, and its widths (16, 144 and 2304 dims) differ from V-JEPA 2 (1024) and DINOv2 (1536). The pixel baseline uses the same 8-frame time bins as Cosmos's moving slots and still reaches 0.982 for μ, so temporal compression alone does not explain the gap. The generator itself, the diffusion transformer, was not probed. Esmati et al. report that physical plausibility is poorly decodable from video-VAE latents and emerges inside the denoiser; here ln m (0.943) does survive in the latent space a video diffusion model generates in. Whether the missing part of μ is lost or only nonlinear was not tested (no MLP probe, no decoding of the latents).

Both studies find raw pixels at least as good as the pretrained encoders in a clean scene, and frame-averaged DINOv2 far below V-JEPA 2 for friction. CALIPER's Concat. column already shows the aggregation effect behind points 3 and 4: keeping the frames apart instead of averaging them raises friction R² for DINOv2 (0.291 → 0.451) and for a random ViT (0.361 → 0.672), as it does here (DINOv2 0.176 → 0.842). CALIPER also compares against a ceiling set by the true simulator state, and its erasure analysis shows that linear decodability does not mean a model uses the information; this study measures decodability only. What we add is the slide negative control, the separation of the unquantised x(t) reference (a ceiling for μ only) from its 1-px quantised version, the generative-tokenizer latent and the contact channel split into oracle and realistic signals.

## Sanity gates

Summaries: push mean PASS, static PASS, null PASS, oracle PASS, slide PASS, poscheck PASS, contact_label PASS; plan v1 rules (informational): shuffle_v1 PASS, oracle_v1 FAIL; slide mean PASS, static PASS, null PASS, oracle PASS, slide PASS, poscheck PASS, contact_label PASS; plan v1 rules (informational): shuffle_v1 PASS; poscheck mean PASS, static N/A, null PASS, oracle N/A, slide PASS, poscheck PASS, contact_label PASS; plan v1 rules (informational): shuffle_v1 FAIL (`results/<dataset>/gates.md`). The gates are defined in CONTRACT v1.3:

- the mean predictor's R² lies in [3·E − 0.01, 0.01], with E = −(1 + n_test/n_train)/(n_test − 3) its expected value for Gaussian targets (the printed p-values are approximate: the three splits reshuffle the same clips);
- every static (frame 0) input has R² < 0.05;
- the label-permutation null has p95 ≤ max(0.10, 3.3/√n_train) and mean ≤ 0.02 for every input;
- the unquantised x(t) reaches R² > 0.95 for ln m and > 0.9 for μ on push, and > 0.9 for μ on slide;
- every video and pixel input has R²(ln m) < 0.05 on slide;
- each encoder keeps position, R²(x₀) ≥ 0.5, in its pooling chosen on val;
- raw contact is labelled as an oracle.

On slide, μ is 0.999–1.000 for the pixel clip inputs and every V-JEPA 2 mode, 0.967 and 0.999 for the Cosmos spatial-mean and grid modes, and 0.761 for the Cosmos 16-dim global mean; static inputs give −0.001. DINOv2 and the random-init controls are listed in `results/slide/table.md`.

## Protocol sensitivity (cross-check of two implementations)

The canonical pipeline (PCA to 256 components when D > 2048, α by 5-fold CV over 10⁻³…10⁶) and the quick-look implementation (PCA to 512 only when D > 4096, α ≤ 10⁵ by GCV) agree within 0.007 on push and slide for every input of at most 2048 dims (poscheck: Cosmos global mean 0.158 vs 0.191) and within 0.001 on slide μ for every video input. On slide ln m the high-dimensional sequence inputs differ by up to 0.026 (V-JEPA 2 `clip_seq_L16`: −0.030 canonical, −0.056 quick-look), with every value ≤ 0. They differ in R²(μ) for inputs that pass through PCA: V-JEPA 2 `clip_seq` 0.967 vs 0.975; DINOv2 `clip_seq` 0.842 vs 0.891; random-init V-JEPA 2 `clip_seq` 0.883 vs 0.936; random-init DINOv2 `clip_seq` 0.684 vs 0.801. The Cosmos grid (2304 dims) is reduced to 256 components in the canonical run and not reduced at all in the quick-look: 0.682 vs 0.748. High-dimensional sequence features are under-read at 256 components, so comparisons across representations should be made at a fixed pooled width (the `clip_mean` rows: 1024, 1536 or 16 dims, no PCA) or at a matched PCA budget. The size of the pretraining gain depends on this choice (point 4).

## Limitations

- **One synthetic scene.** One untextured cube, a grey floor, one light and one fixed top-down camera. The results show what is readable in principle, not robustness.
- **Dry-friction model.** Coulomb friction on the cube's translational degrees of freedom: no rotation, no tipping. The contact channel is the floor's reaction under that model.
- **Identifiability by construction.** Mass is recoverable only because the impulse is fixed and known.
- **The clean scene does not rank models.** Pixels match the best encoder, and gaps of 0.005–0.01 are below the protocol sensitivity.
- **Protocol sensitivity.** PCA width and α selection move R²(μ) of PCA-reduced inputs by up to 0.12 in the released run (random-init DINOv2 `clip_seq` 0.684 vs 0.801; Cosmos grid 0.682 vs 0.748; DINOv2 + touch gain +0.11 vs +0.08).
- **Linear decodability, not use.** No MLP row, so nonlinear readability is not measured, and decodability does not show that a model uses the information (cf. CALIPER's erasure analysis).
- **No confidence intervals beyond the spread over 3 splits.** The splits reshuffle one pool of 2000 clips, and there is no paired bootstrap of R² differences.
- **Cosmos settings.** The tokenizer was trained at 720p and is fed 256 px; it runs in bf16 only; its latents are raw and pooled only to 1×1 and 4×4. The shipped `mean_std.pt` normalisation is constant over space, so per-feature z-scoring cancels it for `clip_seq` and `clip_grid4` but not exactly for `clip_mean`.
- **"Generative" framing.** The only generative-model component is the Cosmos tokenizer's encoder. V-JEPA 2 is a predictive, non-generative self-supervised model and DINOv2 an image self-supervised model. The generator itself was not probed.

## Caveats and known issues

- **The 1-px-quantised x(t) is not an upper bound.** Block-averaged 32×32 pixels keep sub-pixel position through intensity blending, and heavy high-friction cubes move only 3–5 px in total, so the quantised x(t) (0.865) under-reads μ while pixels (0.982) and the unquantised x(t) (1.000) do not.
- **The unquantised x(t) is a ceiling for μ only.** For ln m, pixels and V-JEPA 2 beat it (0.999 and 0.997 vs 0.976), because ln m = ln J − ln v₀ is nonlinear in x(t). Its "fraction of oracle" above 1 is printed as `n/a (>1)`.
- **Single label-shuffle controls** can reach R² ≈ 0.2 on the small poscheck set (n_train = 210) by chance correlation of the permuted labels with the true ones. The canonical pipeline therefore uses a 100-permutation null with an n-aware bound.
- **Plan vs delivered (proposal v2, section 4).** Minimum: all delivered (data, the four encoders and Cosmos, baselines, ridge probes over 3 splits, tables, headline and scatter figures, repository), plus the slide control, the demo pair of a 0.6 kg and a 1.8 kg cube and published features for a CPU-only reproduction. Optional A: the realistic touch signal and its noise curve are done; visual nuisances (`push_nuis`), the clip-length curve, MLP probes, bootstrap intervals with paired tests, out-of-range extrapolation and SigLIP2 are not. Optional B: not done (Wan2.2 VAE and DiT hidden states, DINOv2-large). Also not done: Cosmos at 8×8 or full latent resolution and with the shipped normalisation, a sample-efficiency curve, and a layer sweep beyond blocks 8, 16 and 24.
